Symbols marked *logical* are not hardware: read them, then skip them in the 3D model. Where a symbol has a component, that is the registry name to use.

## Valves

| **Gate valve** `valve-gate` (HV) | Two open triangles meeting tip to tip on the pipe (a bow-tie), with nothing in the middle. A handwheel stem (a T on top) is optional. | Manual isolation valve, normally fully open or fully closed, not for throttling. On PIP/ISA-style legends the plain bow-tie is the gate valve; on ISO 10628 drawings the plain bow-tie is a generic valve, so check the legend. Variants: ISO 10628-2: the plain bow-tie means "valve, general"; Filled (black) bow-tie: normally closed; Some legends add a short vertical bar at the tips to mean gate specifically; Size/class appear only in the line number, not in the symbol | `gate-valve` |
| **Globe valve** `valve-globe` (HV) | Bow-tie with a solid black dot where the triangle tips meet. | Manual throttling valve: regulating flow, control-valve bypasses, and vents and drains that are opened slowly. A globe body with an actuator on top is a control valve (see act-diaphragm). Variants: Some legends draw the dot as a small filled circle with a stem and handwheel; Bypass globe valves around a control valve are normally closed: the body or dot may be filled; Look-alikes: open circle = ball valve, small rectangle = plug valve | `globe-valve` |
| **Ball valve** `valve-ball` (HV) | Bow-tie with an open (white) circle at the centre, where the tips meet. | Quarter-turn isolation valve: full-bore isolation, pigging lines, ESD and on/off duty when actuated. Small sizes have a lever, large ones a gear. Variants: Full-bore vs reduced-bore is usually written as FB / RB next to the symbol; Trunnion vs floating is not shown, only on the valve datasheet; Look-alikes: filled dot = globe valve, small rectangle = plug valve | `ball-valve` |
| **Ball valve, encircled style** `valve-ball-encircled` (HV) | A circle large enough to enclose the bow-tie, so it reads as a circle with an X in it, usually between two flange bars on the line. | Ball valve in legends that draw the ball as a full circle around the bow-tie (common on European and NORSOK-style drawings). Maps to the ball-valve component; a filled bow-tie or NC beside it means normally closed. Variants: Check the sheet legend: a few legends use the encircled X for a general or special valve; Look-alike: a circle with an X on an electrical or lighting drawing is a lamp, not a valve | `ball-valve` |
| **Butterfly valve** `valve-butterfly` (HV) | Two short vertical lines across the pipe (wafer body between flanges) with a diagonal line through the middle (the disc) and a dot at its centre (the shaft). | Quarter-turn disc valve used for large low-pressure lines: seawater, cooling water, firewater and HVAC. Can isolate or throttle. Variants: Very common alternative (PIP-style): a bow-tie with a vertical line through the centre and a dot; Some legends draw the disc as a thin ellipse instead of a line; Triple-offset (metal-seated) types are only identified on the datasheet | `butterfly-valve` |
| **Plug valve** `valve-plug` (HV) | Bow-tie with a small upright rectangle (the plug) at the centre. | Quarter-turn valve with a tapered or cylindrical plug, often lubricated. Used for isolation in sandy or dirty service and on wellhead / manifold duties. Variants: Some legends fill the rectangle; Multi-port plug valves are drawn with three or four triangles (see the 3-way valve); Look-alikes: open circle = ball valve, filled dot = globe valve | `plug-valve` |
| **Needle valve** `valve-needle` (HV) | Small bow-tie with a stem carrying a solid triangle pointing down onto the body (the needle), topped by a handwheel bar. | Fine-throttling instrument valve, ½" and below: instrument roots, gauge isolation, sample and purge lines. Usually not modelled at plant scale. Variants: Often drawn without the needle as a plain small bow-tie on an instrument impulse line; Instrument manifolds (2-, 3-, 5-valve) are drawn as boxes or groups of small valves | `needle-valve` |
| **Diaphragm valve** `valve-diaphragm` (HV) | Bow-tie with a curved arc (the diaphragm bonnet) spanning the top of the body where the tips meet, and a stem above. | Valve that closes by pressing an elastomer diaphragm onto a weir. Used for corrosive, slurry or chemical-injection service. No dedicated 3D component; use globe-valve and note the real type. Variants: Some legends draw a small dome or a horizontal bar above the tips; Weir type vs straight-through is only on the datasheet | – |
| **Pinch valve** `valve-pinch` (HV) | A flexible sleeve: two curved lines bowing in towards the centre between short end bars, with two solid arrowheads pressing in from above and below. | Valve that closes by squeezing a rubber sleeve. Used for slurry, sand and abrasive service. Rare offshore. No dedicated component; use a generic valve and note it. Variants: Legends vary widely: some show a bow-tie with "P" or "PINCH" beside it; Air-operated pinch valves show the air connection to the body | – |
| **Knife gate valve** `valve-knife-gate` (HV) | Bow-tie with a thin upright rectangle (the knife blade) through the centre that rises above the body, then a stem and handwheel bar. | Gate valve with a sharpened blade that cuts through solids. Used for slurry, sand, drains and low-pressure bulk lines. Variants: Some legends draw only a vertical line through the tips; Look-alike: needle valve has a solid triangle on the stem, not a blade | `knife-gate-valve` |
| **Angle valve** `valve-angle` (HV) | Two triangles at right angles: one pointing in from the side (inlet) and one pointing up from below (outlet), tips meeting, with a stem and handwheel on top. | Globe-type valve whose outlet is at 90° to the inlet. Common for drains, chokes and blowdown. A spring on top makes it a relief valve, not an angle valve. Variants: A dot at the tips makes it an angle globe valve; With an actuator it is an angle control valve; With a spring or a pilot box it is a PSV (see relief) | – |
| **Three-way valve** `valve-3way` (HV, TV, XV) | Three triangles meeting tip to tip: left, right and one pointing up from below, each with a pipe stub. Usually carries an actuator or handwheel. | Diverting or mixing valve, e.g. a cooling-medium bypass (TCV) or a PSV changeover. With an actuator, arrows on the ports show the failed flow path. No dedicated component; use ball-valve or control-valve and note it. Variants: A filled triangle marks the port that is normally closed; PSV changeover (interlocked) valves are often drawn as a pair of 3-way valves joined by a dashed mechanical link; Plug-type 3-way bodies show a small rectangle at the centre | – |
| **Four-way valve** `valve-4way` (HV, XV) | Four triangles meeting at one point, like two bow-ties crossed, with a pipe stub on every side. | Four-port diverter: flow reversal (e.g. backflushing a filter or a meter prover diverter). No dedicated component. Variants: Filled triangles mark closed ports; Prover four-way diverters are often drawn as a box labelled "4-way" | – |
| **Normally closed valve** `valve-normally-closed` (HV) | Bow-tie with both triangles filled solid black. | The valve is closed in normal operation: drains, bypasses, crossovers, spare connections. Read the body type from the legend; the fill only means closed. Set the valve normally closed in the model. Variants: Some legends fill only the inner part, or write NC beside an open bow-tie; Filled globe, ball or plug valves keep their centre mark; A few old legends use filled bodies for check valves instead, so check the legend | `gate-valve` |
| **Locked open valve** `valve-locked-open` (HV) | A valve body with the letters "LO" written beside or above it. | The valve is locked in the open position, for example the isolation valves under a PSV or on a vent, so it cannot be shut by mistake. Model it as normally open and note "LO". The lock or seal is a padlock or chain; the body type is read from the bow-tie, and gate-valve is only the default. Variants: The letters can be below the body or at the end of a leader; Some legends add a small padlock or a "C" hook; Ball valves with LO/LC have an open circle in the bow-tie | `gate-valve` |
| **Locked closed valve** `valve-locked-closed` (HV) | A valve body with the letters "LC" written beside or above it. The body is often drawn filled because the valve is closed. | The valve is locked closed, for example a drain or a spool to a flare, so it cannot be opened by mistake. Model it as normally closed and note "LC". The lock or seal is a padlock or chain; the body type is read from the bow-tie, and gate-valve is only the default. Variants: The letters can be below the body or at the end of a leader; Some legends add a small padlock or a "C" hook; Ball valves with LO/LC have an open circle in the bow-tie | `gate-valve` |
| **Car-sealed open valve** `valve-car-sealed-open` (HV) | A valve body with the letters "CSO" written beside or above it. | The valve is sealed open with a car seal. Breaking the seal needs a permit, and seals are audited. Typical on PSV inlet and outlet isolations. Model it as normally open and note "CSO". The lock or seal is a numbered car seal (wire tag); the body type is read from the bow-tie, and gate-valve is only the default. Variants: The letters can be below the body or at the end of a leader; Some legends add a small padlock or a "C" hook; Ball valves with LO/LC have an open circle in the bow-tie | `gate-valve` |
| **Car-sealed closed valve** `valve-car-sealed-closed` (HV) | A valve body with the letters "CSC" written beside or above it. The body is often drawn filled because the valve is closed. | The valve is sealed closed with a car seal. Model it as normally closed and note "CSC". The lock or seal is a numbered car seal (wire tag); the body type is read from the bow-tie, and gate-valve is only the default. Variants: The letters can be below the body or at the end of a leader; Some legends add a small padlock or a "C" hook; Ball valves with LO/LC have an open circle in the bow-tie | `gate-valve` |
| **Float-operated valve** `valve-float` (LCV, LV) | Bow-tie with a stem rising to a horizontal lever that ends in a circle (the float). | Self-acting level valve (ball-cock): tank make-up water, diesel day tank fill, drain pots. No instrument loop; the float moves the valve directly. Variants: Sometimes drawn with a dashed mechanical link from the valve to a float inside the tank; Tagged LCV (self-actuated level control valve) | – |
| **Double block and bleed** `valve-double-block-bleed` (HV) | Two isolation valves in series on the pipe with a small bleed valve on a branch between them, often inside a dashed box when it is a single-body DBB valve. | Positive isolation for maintenance: both blocks are shut and the cavity between them is bled to a safe place. Required on high-pressure hydrocarbon isolations and instrument connections. Model the block valves as ball-valve or gate-valve and list the bleed as not modelled. Variants: Single-body DBB valves (two balls in one body) are drawn as one box with two bow-ties; The bleed may go to a closed drain or a vent line; Instrument DBB manifolds are often shown as a single rectangle labelled DBB | – |
| **Damper (louvre)** `valve-damper` (HD, FD, MD) | A rectangle across the duct with several parallel slanted blades inside. | Duct damper on HVAC, fired-heater stack or fan systems. A fire damper (FD) closes on fire; a gas-tight damper isolates enclosures on gas detection. No dedicated component. Variants: Single-blade dampers look like a butterfly valve on a duct; Fire dampers may be marked FD or carry a fusible-link note; Motorised dampers carry an M actuator | – |
| **Fixed (positive) choke** `choke-fixed` (CH, FO) | An angle body (inlet triangle from the side, outlet triangle below) with a plain cap on top instead of a handwheel, and an orifice (a split bar) across the outlet. | Wellhead or manifold choke with a fixed-size bean (orifice). Changing rate means changing the bean, so there is no handwheel. Sits on the christmas-tree wing outlet. Variants: Some legends draw a bow-tie with "CH" or a bean (small black rectangle) inside; Bean size is written as a fraction of an inch in 64ths, e.g. 32/64" | `choke-valve` |
| **Adjustable choke** `choke-adjustable` (HCV, CH, PCV) | An angle body with a handwheel stem and a long diagonal arrow through the body (the ISO mark for "adjustable"). | Wellhead choke whose opening is set by hand or by an actuator (with an actuator it is a choke control valve, often HCV or PCV). Controls well rate and drops pressure from wellhead to manifold. Variants: Actuated chokes carry a stepping actuator or an M motor; Some legends draw a bow-tie with a diagonal arrow; Choke position may be shown as % or 64ths | `choke-valve` |

Found on pump discharges, compressor discharges and anywhere back-flow must be stopped. The filled side points in the flow direction. Swing type is the default when the legend does not say. Suited to high pressure, small bore and pulsating flow (reciprocating pumps and compressors). | **Ball check valve** `check-ball` (NRV) | An open triangle pointing downstream at a small black ball, which sits against a vertical bar (the seat). | Check valve with a free ball. Used on small lines, metering-pump discharges and chemical injection. No dedicated component; use check-valve-piston if it must be modelled. Variants: Some legends draw a bow-tie with a filled circle in the outlet side; Injection check valves at the quill are often drawn as this symbol | – |
| **Dual-plate check valve** `check-dual-plate` (NRV) | Two vertical bars (a wafer between flanges) with two short plates hinged at the centre and opening downstream in a "<" shape, and a flow arrow above. | Wafer check valve with two spring-loaded half-discs on a central hinge pin. Short and light, so common on compressor and pump discharges and on large water lines. Variants: Many legends use the general check symbol and write "dual plate" or "DPC"; Some draw a single vertical bar with a filled triangle | `dual-plate-check-valve` |
| **Stop-check valve** `check-stop` (NRV) | A lift check (bow-tie, outlet filled, open circle at the centre) with a stem and handwheel on top, and a flow arrow beside it. | Screw-down non-return valve: acts as a check valve, but the handwheel can also hold it shut. Used on boiler feeds and parallel pump discharges. No dedicated component; use check-valve-piston and note the handwheel. Variants: Some legends draw a globe valve with an arrow and a handwheel | – |

## Relief, regulators and safety devices

| Symbol | Drawn as | Meaning | Component |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pressure safety valve (spring loaded)** `psv-spring` (PSV, PRV, TSV, SV, PSV, PRV, TSV) | An angle body with the inlet from below and the outlet to the side, and a zig-zag spring on top capped by a short bar. A PSV bubble with the loop number usually sits beside it (not drawn here), with the set pressure and the orifice size (e.g. "3L4"). | Last-line overpressure protection. It lifts at the set pressure and relieves to flare, vent or atmosphere. Inlet size, orifice letter and outlet size read "3L4" = 3" inlet, L orifice, 4" outlet. TSVs are small thermal-relief valves on blocked-in liquid lines. Variants: Bellows-balanced PSVs have a small bellows drawn under the spring; Some legends draw a straight-through body with a spring; PSVs often sit between LO/CSO isolation valves, with a spare PSV on a changeover valve | `pressure-safety-valve` |
| **Pilot-operated relief valve** `psv-pilot` (PSV, PSV) | An angle relief body with a piston chamber (a box) on top instead of a spring, and a small spring-loaded pilot beside it with a sensing line back to the inlet. | Relief valve whose main piston is held shut by inlet pressure and released by a small pilot. Tight up to the set point, so used where operating pressure is close to set pressure (gas export, large flare loads). Variants: The pilot can be drawn as a small PSV symbol or a box with "P"; Remote-sensing pilots have the sensing line run to the vessel, not the valve inlet | `pressure-safety-valve` |
| **Pressure-vacuum (breather) valve** `breather-valve` (PVRV, PSV) | A housing on a tank nozzle with two arrows inside: one pointing up (pressure relief out) and one pointing down (vacuum relief in), each over a weighted pallet. | Tank breather valve on atmospheric tanks (API 650/2000). It vents vapour when the tank fills or heats and lets air in when it empties. No dedicated component; note it on the storage-tank. Variants: Vacuum breakers alone show only the down arrow; Often combined with a flame arrester below it; Some legends draw a PSV symbol with "P/V" beside it | – |
| **Rupture disc (bursting disc)** `rupture-disc` (PSE, RD, BD, PSE) | Two short parallel bars (the holder) across a vertical pipe, with a curved arc between them bulging towards the outlet. | Non-reclosing relief device that bursts at a set pressure. Used alone, or under a PSV to protect it from corrosion (then a pressure gauge or PI is drawn between them). ISA tag is PSE. Maps to the rupture-disc component. Variants: Some legends draw a vertical pipe with a dashed or zig-zag line across it; Buckling-pin relief valves are drawn as a body with a pin and tagged PSE too | `rupture-disc` |
| **Flame arrester** `flame-arrester` (FA) | An in-line rectangle on the pipe with zig-zag lines inside (the crimped-metal element). | Passive device that stops a flame front from passing down a vent line or into a tank. Found on tank vents, drain-tank vents and on vent stacks. No dedicated component. Variants: Detonation arresters are drawn the same and marked "DA"; Some legends use a rectangle with crossed diagonal hatching | – |
| **Self-actuated pressure regulator (PCV)** `regulator-pressure` (PCV, PCV) | A bow-tie body with a diaphragm dome on its stem, a spring above the dome, and a thin sensing line from the dome to the pipe downstream of the valve. | Pressure-reducing regulator with no instrument loop: downstream pressure acts on the diaphragm directly. Fuel gas, instrument air, nitrogen and blanket gas. Tag PCV. No dedicated component; control-valve is the closest stand-in. Variants: Internal sensing: no external line is drawn; Some legends draw the sensing line as a dashed or plain thin line; Blanketing regulators sit on a tank roof | – |
| **Back-pressure regulator** `regulator-back-pressure` (PCV, BPR, PCV) | Like the pressure regulator, but the thin sensing line runs from the dome to the pipe upstream of the valve. | Holds upstream pressure: it opens as upstream pressure rises. Used on separator gas outlets to flare, pump minimum-flow and seal systems. No dedicated component. Variants: Some legends write "BPR" beside it; Look at which side the sensing line goes to; that is the controlled side | – |
| **Self-actuated temperature regulator** `regulator-temperature` (TCV, TCV) | A bow-tie body with a diaphragm dome, and a capillary (line with small × marks) running from the dome to a sensing bulb. | Thermostatic valve driven by a filled bulb: lube-oil coolers, tracing and jacket water. No instrument loop and no dedicated component. Variants: The bulb may be drawn as a thermowell in the process line; Wax-element 3-way thermostatic valves are drawn as a 3-way valve with TCV | – |
| **Excess-flow valve** `excess-flow-valve` (EFV) | A bow-tie with a spring drawn inside the inlet triangle and a flow arrow above. | Closes automatically when flow exceeds a set rate (a line break downstream). Found on instrument connections, LPG and chemical lines. No dedicated component. Variants: Usually only a check-valve-like symbol tagged EFV; Some legends write "XS" or "EFV" next to a plain bow-tie | – |
